# N2-T2 model-scaling fleet, September 2026: every model, every number, every checkpoint

_Written 2026-09-18 on the 8×A100 box (hep-ssl-inar). Source of the numbers: `evals/ladder/final_table.csv`, generated by `scripts/final_table.py` from the per-event prediction files. Run definitions and the paired comparisons that led to each conclusion: `evals/ladder/fleet_table.md`._

## 1. What was done and why

The paper's draft said model scaling saturates (a 19M-parameter model, 6 layers wide, did no better than the 5M one). A 10M-event ladder on the KU cluster suggested that was a width artefact and that **depth** scales. This fleet tested that at full data (130M training events) for both DOM tokenizations, then ran the follow-ups the results called for (input embedding, dropout, learning rate, seeds, 36 layers, the hi-E finetune recipe) and finally scored every model on the held-out test batches.

**Data and protocol.** Kaggle IceCube data at `/data/inar/icecube_kaggle`. Train = batches 1–650 (130M events); dev = batch 651 (first 200k events; used for checkpoint selection and per-epoch curves); validation = batches 652–655 (800k events; every model selection and every paired comparison below); test = batches 656–659 (800k events; scored once per model at the end, after all selections were made, following the repo's convention; the two headline models were additionally selected under a rule written down before the test was run, recorded in `fleet_table.md`). Angular errors are in degrees; Kaggle's metric is the same mean in radians (55.39° = 0.967 rad).

**Model.** One flat transformer over DOM tokens (`src/iceaggr/models/flat_transformer_v2.py`): d_model 256, 8 heads, FFN 1024, at most 128 DOMs per event (512 for finetunes), RMSNorm, QK-norm, ReLU², zero-init projections, dropout 0.1 unless stated. Two tokenizations: **raw** = the first 84 pulses (time, charge, aux) plus x, y, z, n_pulses = 256 numbers per DOM; **hybrid** = the first 80 pulses plus 12 hand-computed per-DOM statistics (the July record model's tokenization). Input embedding: **none** (the 256-vector enters the residual stream directly) or **learned linear** (a trained 256→256 layer; every hybrid run uses it). Head: 3-component von Mises–Fisher mixture, unclamped κ, κ² penalty annealed to zero over the first 30% of steps; loss = mixture NLL + 0.5 × angular distance.

**Optimisation.** Muon (momentum 0.95, weight decay 0.01) on all block weight matrices, AdamW (lr 3e-4) on CLS/head/scalars/input layer; OneCycle with 2000 warmup steps; bf16; gradient clip 1.0; effective batch 4096 (130M runs, micro-batch by depth with gradient accumulation) or 1024 (10M runs); 10 epochs (130M) or 5 (10M). One run (F21, in progress) uses plain AdamW instead of Muon.

**Hi-E finetune recipe** (final, after the checks in §4): continue from a base's final checkpoint with a fresh optimizer, on events with ≥500 pulses (2.56M events), up to 512 DOMs per event, Muon 2.5e-4 / AdamW 3e-5, 500 warmup steps, 10 epochs, effective batch 512, κ penalty off. Dev = the bright events of batch 651.

**How to read the tables.** Base models are scored on all events with 128 DOMs; finetunes on events with ≥200 pulses at their finetune DOM cap. "Selected checkpoint" = lowest dev combined loss (`best.pt`), which is the final epoch for every base run. Seed noise, measured on five seed pairs: 0.01–0.05° on the bulk mean, ≤0.1° on the bright slices; finetune-only noise 0.02°. Bright-event means are heavy-tailed: the sampling uncertainty of an absolute ≥1000 mean on 9k events is ±0.2–0.3°, so only **paired differences on identical events** are used for conclusions, never absolute numbers from different event sets.

## 4. What the fleet established (paired differences on identical validation events; ± = standard error)

**Depth pays at 130M events, and saturates.** Raw tokens, 24 vs 6 layers: −0.10 to −0.16° bulk, −0.52 to −0.56° on ≥1000 pulses, over two seed pairs (F01/F05 vs F02/F12). The curve 6 → 12 → 24 → 36 layers gains −0.09°, −0.07°, ±0.03° on the bulk and −0.40°, −0.16°, ±0.05° on bright events: 24 layers is the operating point (F06, 36 layers, equals 24 within seed noise). On hybrid tokens depth saturates at 12 (F15 = F07/F10 within 0.03°). No run overfits: every train–dev gap is negative.

**A learned linear input layer is worth as much as doubling the depth.** On the 6-layer raw model −0.11° bulk / −0.42° on ≥1000 (F11 vs F02); on top of 24 layers a further −0.09° / −0.24° (F03 vs F01). The 2×2 (F02, F11, F01, F03) is additive: depth + linear = −0.25° bulk, −0.80° on ≥1000, and the same additivity holds after finetuning (each lever −0.32° on ≥1000; F02ft 5.83 → F11ft 5.50 / F01ft 5.51 → F03ft 5.19).

**Hybrid statistics vs raw pulses.** The hybrid tokenization costs 0.6° on the bulk at every depth and buys 0.3° on ≥1000 before finetuning. After the finetune the bright-event edge shrinks to 0.05–0.13° (hybrid pair F07ft/F10ft vs raw pair F03ft/F20ft, both finetuned on ≥1000: on validation +0.02 to +0.13, on test +0.02 to +0.13, one to two standard errors), while the raw finetunes lead by 0.2–0.4° on 200–999 pulses (on test F20ft vs F07ft/F10ft: −0.32 / −0.21 ± 0.08). Depth pays for hybrid too (−0.12 to −0.15° bulk, −0.26 to −0.33° on ≥1000), half the raw gain. Conclusion: the hand-computed statistics compensated for a shallow model; with depth and a learned input layer raw pulses match them on the brightest events and win everywhere else.

**Dropout 0.1 helps only the small models.** Removing it gains 0.07–0.11° bulk and 0.24° on ≥1000 at 6 layers on raw tokens (F14 vs F02/F12; and −0.17° after finetuning), 0.06° at 6 layers on hybrid (F13), and nothing at 24 layers on either tokenization, before or after finetuning (F19 vs F03: +0.02 ± 0.01°; F18 vs F07/F10: −0.04/−0.01°; F19ft vs F03ft: +0.03; F18ft vs F07ft: +0.01).

**Muon learning rate.** 0.0035 and 0.005 are indistinguishable at 24 layers on both tokenizations (F09 vs F01: −0.02 ± 0.01° bulk; F17 vs F07/F10: −0.04/0.00). The 10M ladder's preference for 0.0035 did not carry over; the mid-run dev-NLL jitter at 0.005 costs nothing at the end.

**Optimizer.** The depth gain depends on Muon. F21, the exact AdamW twin of the headline model F20 (24 layers, learned input layer, seed 41, AdamW 3e-4 throughout), trails it by +0.17 ± 0.01° on the bulk and +0.47 to +0.55° on ≥1000 pulses on both validation and test, and lands exactly on the Muon 6-layer-plus-linear model F11 (−0.01 / 0.00° bulk). It still beats the plain Muon 6-layer control by 0.12–0.14° bulk, so depth helps under AdamW too, but at half the size, and a 6-layer Muon model already matches a 24-layer AdamW one. This also explains part of the paper's original "saturation" finding, which used AdamW.

**Seeds.** Five base seed pairs (F02/F12, F01/F05, F03/F20, F07/F10, F08/F16): 0.01–0.05° on the bulk, ≤0.1° on the slices (the largest, F03/F20 on ≥1000, 0.12°). Finetune-only replicate (F03ft seeds): 0.02°. Base+finetune pairs (F07ft/F10ft, F01ft/F05ft, F08ft/F16ft): 0.02–0.06°.

**Old vs new on identical events.** The July record recipe (hybrid 5M base + ≥1000 finetune) re-run under this protocol reproduces its July dev number (5.75° vs 5.76°) and scores 5.29° on the validation bright events, 5.71° on test. The new finetunes beat it by 0.11–0.22° on ≥1000 (validation) and by 0.15–0.25° on test; the raw deep base beats its base by 0.7° on the bulk. Absolute bright-event numbers must not be compared across event sets (the validation batches' bright events are ~0.4° easier than the July validation set and ~0.4° easier than the test batches).

**Finetune recipe checks, all on F03's base.** Training set ≥500 vs ≥1000 pulses: same on ≥1000 (−0.01 ± 0.03°), −0.17° better on 200–999 → use ≥500. 1024 vs 512 DOMs: identical, even on ≥5000-pulse events (0.00 ± 0.01°) → 512. Muon lr 1.25e-4 / 2.5e-4 / 5e-4: flat within 0.05° → 2.5e-4. 20 vs 10 epochs: +0.05 to +0.07° worse and the train–dev gap grows from +0.12 to +0.45 → 10 epochs. The finetune improves every event above 200 pulses and costs +0.13 to +0.22° on the dim majority, so a two-model deployment (base below 200 pulses, finetune above) is used.

**Validation → test.** Every base model scores 0.06–0.11° higher on test than on validation, every finetune ~0.4° higher on ≥1000 (harder test bright events), with all orderings preserved. Nothing was selected on the test set.

## 7. Infrastructure notes (what changed in the code, and why)

- `src/iceaggr/utils/muon.py`: the Muon step buckets matrices by shape and runs one batched Newton–Schulz per bucket with fused `_foreach` updates (was a Python loop over 144 matrices: 89.6 ms → 6.5 ms per step). State-dict layout unchanged; equivalence tests in `tests/unit/test_muon.py`.
- `scripts/train_flat.py`: running sums kept on the GPU (no per-step `.item()` syncs), `non_blocking` host-to-device copies, `data.prefetch_factor` and `data.eval_workers` configurable, checkpoint dir created with parents. Together with the Muon change this took the jobs from 72–82% to 97–100% GPU utilisation at unchanged numerics (smoke runs agree to bf16 noise).
- `scripts/a100_fleet_20260903.sh`: counts all cores (login shells here are pinned to 64 of 255 by numad), `SCREEN=1` worker mode, a `stop` mode that signals only the real worker processes, and `extra`…`extra7` functions recording every run added after the original fleet. `scripts/queue_worker.sh` also traps HUP. `scripts/ladder_summary.py` skips runs killed before their first epoch. `scripts/final_table.py` builds the tables in this document.
- Operational lessons: never more than ~36 loader workers per job and lower `prefetch_factor` before adding workers (pinned-memory churn is a box-wide cost that starves other jobs' transfer threads); 6-layer finetunes on bright events are loader-bound (~80% GPU) and that is accepted; small models with heavy events need more eval-loader workers than the default 2.
